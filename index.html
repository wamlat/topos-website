Abstract

We exhibit a laboratory \(\mathcal{L}\) with the following universal property: for every hard problem \(P\), there exists a factorization of \(P\) through \(\mathcal{L}\) into local pieces, each merely difficult, together with the coherence data required to glue their solutions back into a whole. We call this the working sheaf condition. Applications to software are discussed. The proof is constructive; it is this website.

1Definitions

Definition 1.1 (topos). A topos is a category \(\mathcal{E}\) that behaves like the category of sheaves of sets on a space, \(\mathcal{E} \simeq \mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)\): it has all finite limits, it is cartesian closed, and it possesses a subobject classifier \(\Omega\) — a single object that knows, for every object \(X\), every way of carving a part out of \(X\).

The slogan, in the spirit of Grothendieck: a topos is a universe in which one can do mathematics. Change the site and you change the universe — but the logic comes along for the ride. We build software the same way: choose the site carefully, and the rest follows by general nonsense. “General nonsense” is what category theorists affectionately call their own machinery. It is the highest compliment we know.

Definition 1.2 (Topos Labs). Topos Labs is a small research and software studio. Its objects are systems; its morphisms are the maps that preserve what matters. It is not, as far as we can verify, cartesian closed — but it is finitely complete, in the sense that we finish what we start.

2The sheaf condition

A sheaf \(F\) on a space \(X\) assigns data to every open set, subject to one law: whenever local data agree on the overlaps, they glue — uniquely — into global data. For a cover \(U = \bigcup_i U_i\), the diagram

$$F(U) \longrightarrow \prod_i F(U_i) \rightrightarrows \prod_{i,j} F(U_i \cap U_j) \tag{1}$$

is an equalizer. This single line is the entire philosophy of the laboratory. Most failures of engineering — and of organizations — are failures of the sheaf condition: locally excellent work that disagrees on the overlaps. So we design the overlaps first. A presheaf is what you have before anyone checks that the pieces agree. Much of the software industry ships presheaves.

3What the laboratory makes

Example 3.1 (systems). Software whose parts were designed to agree on their overlaps: interfaces, protocols, infrastructure. Locality is a feature; coherence is the product.

Example 3.2 (instruments). Tools that let a person hold more structure in their head than fits there naturally. A good instrument is a functor from a hard category to an easy one, faithful where it counts.

Example 3.3 (research). Questions held long enough to become tools. We publish what generalizes.

Remark. The laboratory is small by construction. Small sites can present large topoi.

4Morphisms welcome

The laboratory admits maps in and out. To construct one, write to hello@toposlabs.com with a description of your source object. We read everything that commutes.
